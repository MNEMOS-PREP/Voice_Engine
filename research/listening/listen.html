Which interviewer sounds like a person?

The same lines in each candidate voice. "Today" is the voice students hear now, played live by your browser: open this page in Microsoft Edge for it.

Names shown

The Indian voices are six speakers of IndicTTS (SMT Lab, IIT Madras; CC BY 4.0), English recorded by native speakers of Indian languages for speech synthesis, cloned from about 15 seconds each; chosen as the most different of the 21 distinct voices sampled. "Higher" and "lower" describe the pitch of each voice, nothing more.

The foreign cloned voices are DailyTalk's two speakers (Lee et al., ICASSP 2023; CC BY-SA 4.0), recorded for conversational speech-synthesis research, cloned here from about 12 seconds of their own conversation. Research only: a dataset licence is not a speaker's consent to become a product voice. Chatterbox marks every clip with an inaudible watermark.

"Written" lines are what the interviewer writes today; "spoken" lines say the same thing the way people talk. Comparing the two shows how much is the voice and how much is the wording. Under each clip: how long this laptop's CPU took to make it. Every clip plays at normal speed; a voice that takes longer to make than to play cannot answer live without a faster computer.
