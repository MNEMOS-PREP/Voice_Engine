# The interviewers' voices — where each comes from

Each file here is a ~15-second reference recording. Chatterbox Turbo (Resemble
AI, MIT) speaks new lines in the voice of its reference; it does not train on
it. Every voice below is used within its licence, credited as the licence asks,
and presented to students as an **AI interviewer** — never as the person
recorded, and never as anyone real.

| Voice | File | Source | Licence | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indian voices 1 and 3-13 | `indictts-*.flac` | IndicTTS — English recorded by native speakers of Indian languages **for speech synthesis**; a MeitY-funded consortium led by IIT Madras | CC BY 4.0 | IndicTTS, SMT Lab, IIT Madras |
| Conversational voice A | `dailytalk-0.flac` | DailyTalk — dialogues recorded for conversational text-to-speech | CC BY-SA 4.0, academic use | DailyTalk, Lee et al., ICASSP 2023 |
| Chatterbox voice | `chatterbox-default.flac` | Chatterbox Turbo's built-in voice, re-referenced from its own output | MIT | Chatterbox, Resemble AI |

**Before this platform is used outside an academic setting**, the DailyTalk
voices need replacing or a licence for that use: their release is for academic
use. A dataset licence is also not the speaker's own consent to become a
product's voice (Hugging Face, "Voice cloning with consent") — recordings made
for speech synthesis (IndicTTS, DailyTalk) are the closest fit, and voices
recorded by consenting people for this platform are the best one.

Never add a voice cloned from a celebrity, a character, or anyone who has not
agreed to it (Arijit Singh v. Codible Ventures, Bombay High Court, 2024).
